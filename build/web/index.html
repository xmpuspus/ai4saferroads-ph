Will a lower speed limit actually make us safer?

Not on its own. A lower number no one obeys changes nothing. But the road itself is built for a speed, and people meet it the moment traffic clears. This maps where that speed is higher than a person can survive, next to schools and markets, across 50 cities and Metro Manila.

Road colour
Total crashes peak in rush-hour traffic, when the road is fullest. But the crashes that happen when the road clears are twice as likely to injure or kill.
1 · The harm hides in the clear-road hours
On EDSA, total crashes rise and fall with traffic. But the share of crashes that injure or kill roughly doubles between midnight and 5am. That is when the road finally clears and vehicles reach the speed it is built for. The clear-road hours have less enforcement and more drunk driving too, so this points to speed, it does not prove it alone.
2 · From space, these roads are built for speed
The map data has no road widths here. The satellite shows what the flagged roads are, wide straight multi-lane strips cutting through dense housing. Daytime traffic comes and goes. The road itself stays.
3 · The fix is physical, not a sign
A lower number no one obeys in traffic and no one enforces changes nothing. Only road design that caps speed when the road is empty works. Yet only 20% of Manila's flagged roads have any traffic calming within 100 m, 16% in Cebu, 4% in Davao.
4 · Building for the speed does not mean bending the road
A fast road can stay straight if it keeps people clear of the traffic, a divider down the middle, protected crossings, lanes narrow enough that drivers ease off, and cameras where the limit is ignored. A street beside a school or market is the opposite job, so there you slow the traffic itself, with raised crossings, wider footpaths and a 30 km/h zone. The danger is one road doing both at once, fast like a highway yet lined with homes and stalls. Matching each road to its real use is what road-safety engineers call a Safe System, and the sign still counts once it is enforced or the road is built to back it up.
