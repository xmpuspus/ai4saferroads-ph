# Speed Safety Score — Product Audit: UX + Observability

Scope: `build/web/index.html`, `build/web/sim.html`, `build/web/methodology.html`, `README.md`.
Live: https://ai4saferroads-ph.vercel.app (+ /sim, /methodology). Date of audit: 2026-06-25.

Method: read the four source files line by line, cross-checked `data/cities.json` and `data/corridor.json`, and fetched the live homepage for first-impression. Every finding cites `file:line`.

---

## UX findings (severity-ranked)

| Severity | Finding | Evidence (file:line) | Impact | Concrete fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | No loading state while the 5.5 MB Manila GeoJSON (4.0 MB Cebu, 4.7 MB Hanoi) downloads. The panel renders with empty stats and an empty map until both fetches resolve; on a slow phone connection the user stares at a blank dark map with no spinner, no skeleton, no "loading roads…". | `index.html:180-190` (`loadCity` awaits two large fetches, no pre-fetch UI); data sizes confirmed `data/sss_segments_manila.geojson` = 5.5 MB | First-time visitor on mobile thinks the map is broken and leaves before data arrives. This is the single biggest abandonment risk. | Show a spinner / skeleton stat block immediately on `loadCity` start; hide it in a `finally`. At minimum set the panel stats to a "Loading {city}…" placeholder before the `await`. |
| Critical | A failed fetch fails completely silent and freezes the UI. `loadCity` has no try/catch; if any of `cities.json`, `sss_segments_*`, or `pois_*` 404s or times out, the promise rejects, `renderPanel` never runs, and the user sees a permanently blank map with stale or empty panel and no error message. | `index.html:180-190` (`loadCity` — no try/catch), `index.html:210-214` (initial `cities.json` fetch and first `loadCity` inside `map.on('load')`, also unguarded) | Any CDN hiccup or data-path regression silently bricks the app with zero diagnostic signal to the user or to you. | Wrap `loadCity` body in try/catch; on error render a visible panel message ("Couldn't load {city} road data — retry") with a retry button, and `console.error` the failure. The sim already does this pattern at `sim.html:54`. |
| High | The map DEFAULTS to a 52° tilt + animated traffic dots, and on this dataset the 3D tilt is decoration, not information. The segments are flat 2D lines (`type:'line'`, no extrusion/height), so pitching the camera to 52° only foreshortens and overlaps the very corridors the tool exists to show, while satellite labels smear. The glowing lines carry the signal; the tilt costs legibility for nothing. | `index.html:120` (`pitch:52,bearing:-18` default), `index.html:194-198` (segments are flat `line` layers, no `fill-extrusion`), `index.html:84` (3D is the `.on` default) | "What am I looking at in 5 seconds" is harder than it needs to be: a tilted satellite with overlapping orange lines reads as eye-candy, not a data map. The honest call: default to 2D top-down, offer 3D as opt-in. | Default `pitch:0` (2D) and the `#dim` toggle's `2d` button to `.on`; keep 3D as the explicit choice. If 3D stays default, justify it with actual extrusion (terrain or building height) so the tilt encodes something. |
| High | The animated traffic dots are pure decoration presented as if they were data. They are randomly seeded (`d:Math.random()*len`), move at a fixed fraction of the posted speed (`*0.55`), and represent no real vehicle count, volume, or flow — yet the legend says "Moving dots = traffic on flagged roads." A civic-data tool that animates fake traffic undercuts its own credibility. | `index.html:166` (`d:Math.random()*len`, `spd:(f.properties.v_posted/3.6)*0.55`), `index.html:95-96` (legend calls them "traffic") | A skeptical reader (the exact audience for a road-safety accountability map) will spot that the "traffic" is synthetic and distrust the rest. Borders on the no-mock-data rule. | Either relabel honestly ("animated indicator of relative speed/risk, not measured traffic") or drop the dots and animate the corridor glow intensity instead. Do not call a `Math.random()` particle field "traffic." |
| High | Toggle buttons (Satellite/Dark, 2D/3D, Traffic/Still) do not communicate state to assistive tech. They are plain `<button>` elements that get a visual `.on` class but no `aria-pressed`; the segmented control has no `role="group"` or label. A screen-reader user cannot tell which basemap/dimension/animation is active. | `index.html:83-85` (three `.seg` groups of bare `<button data-v=…>`), `index.html:217-227` (handlers toggle only the `.on` class) | Keyboard/SR users get no state feedback; visually the `.on` orange is the only cue (also a contrast risk, see below). | Add `aria-pressed="true|false"` toggled in the click handlers; wrap each `.seg` in `role="group"` with an `aria-label` ("Basemap", "Dimension", "Traffic"). |
| High | The priority-road list rows are clickable `<div>`s, not keyboard-reachable. Clicking a row flies the camera to that corridor — a core interaction — but the rows have no `tabindex`, no `role="button"`, no `keydown` handler, so a keyboard user cannot trigger the fly-to. | `index.html:43-44` (`.road{…cursor:pointer}` on a `<div>`), `index.html:138-142` (rows rendered as `<div class="road" data-i>`), `index.html:228-232` (click-only handler) | The flagship "jump to the worst road" feature is mouse-only; fails WCAG keyboard-operability. | Render rows as `<button>` (or add `role="button" tabindex="0"` + Enter/Space `keydown`). Move focus to the map region after fly-to. |
| Medium | The sim's color language is red = danger / green = safe with no non-color cue, and the two are near-confusable for red-green colorblind users (~8% of men). The "hits / dies" vs "stops / unharmed" outcomes are distinguished almost entirely by `#ff5a4d` vs `#3ddc97`. | `sim.html:82` (`colors={danger:'#ff5a4d…',safe:'#3ddc97…'}`), `sim.html:122-132` (outcome text colored red/green) | A deuteranope reading the sim may not register which lane is the fatal one — the whole teaching point rides on the color contrast. | Add a redundant cue: an icon/symbol ("✕ struck" vs "✓ stopped"), a textual prefix already present is good but make it color-independent, and consider a colorblind-safe pair (orange/blue). |
| Medium | No empty/zero state is wired into the city panel beyond a single inline fallback. If a city had zero flagged real-posted segments, the road list shows a graceful message — good — but the four headline stats would still render `0`s with no explanation, and there is no handling for a city whose data is present but malformed. | `index.html:142` (road-list fallback string exists), `index.html:132-136` (stats render raw numbers with no zero-state copy) | Edge cities (e.g. a future low-coverage city) read as "broken / all-zero" rather than "this city has thin OSM data." | Add a one-line note under the stats when `segments_flagged_real_posted === 0` ("No posted limit here exceeds the safe speed, or OSM coverage is thin"). |
| Medium | The single `@media (max-width:520px)` block is the only responsive rule and it is not enough. It widens the panel and caps it at 62% height, but the three `.ctrls` toggle rows can still wrap awkwardly, the 356 px panel logic and the `.brand` chip bottom-right can overlap the Maplibre attribution + nav control on a short phone, and the panel at 62% height plus a tilted map leaves little legible map. No tablet breakpoint. | `index.html:69` (lone `@media`), `index.html:24-25` (fixed 356px panel), `index.html:61-62`/`104` (`.brand` bottom-right collides with nav control added at `index.html:121`) | Mobile is the dominant device for a civic share link; the layout is cramped and controls/attribution can overlap. | Add a tablet breakpoint; on mobile collapse the panel to a bottom sheet or make it scroll-dismissable; ensure `.brand` and the nav control don't stack on the same corner at small sizes. |
| Medium | README + the in-app sim button overclaim the sim as "3D / three.js / drive a flagged corridor on real satellite." The actual `sim.html` is a 2D HTML5-canvas side-by-side stopping-distance diagram with cartoon cars — no three.js, no satellite imagery rendered in the sim (`sat_corridor.png` exists in data but the canvas never draws it). | `index.html:88-89` (button: "See why speed kills, in 3D … on real satellite"), `README.md:18,20-23,45-49,68` ("three.js corridor simulation over real satellite imagery", "in 3D", "dives into a flagged arterial"), vs `sim.html:32` (`<canvas>` 2D), `sim.html:59` (`getContext('2d')`), no WebGL/three.js anywhere | First-time visitor clicks "in 3D … on real satellite," lands on a flat 2D diagram, feels misled. Erodes trust on the exact page meant to build it. | Rewrite the button to "See why speed kills — stopping distance at 60 vs 30" and update README to describe the 2D canvas sim accurately. (Task notes the sim was just rewritten from three.js; the copy is stale.) |
| Medium | The Speed Safety Score ramp/legend is hard to decode in 5 seconds. The gradient bar is labeled only "0 aligned / 40 / 75+ worst" with a five-stop color ramp (`#1b3a4b→#ffd166→#ff8c42→#ff3b3b→#b5179e`) that does not visually match the ramp CSS stops, and "aligned" is jargon a first-timer won't parse. The blue POI dots are explained only in a small paragraph below. | `index.html:55-56` (`.ramp` gradient), `index.html:92-96` (labels "0 aligned … 75+ worst" + legend paragraph), `index.html:124` (`SSS_COLOR` interpolation stops differ from the CSS ramp stops) | The core metric the whole site is named after is the least self-explanatory element on screen. | Align the CSS ramp stops to `SSS_COLOR`, replace "aligned" with "limit fits the road," and put the blue-dot key inline on the ramp, not buried in prose. |
| Low | First-impression headline is strong but the 5-second "what am I looking at" relies on reading the sub-paragraph. The `<h1>` "Speed Safety Score" alone doesn't say it's a map of where limits are too high; that lives in the 3-line `.sub`. | `index.html:75-78` (h1 + sub) | Mild: scanners who skip the paragraph miss the thesis. | Add a one-line tagline directly under the h1 ("Where posted limits exceed the speed people survive") at larger weight than the paragraph. |
| Low | No focus-visible styling anywhere; the only interactive elements with native focus are the `<select>` and the sim buttons. With the dark glass theme, default focus rings may be low-contrast or suppressed. | no `:focus`/`:focus-visible` rule in `index.html:14-70` or `sim.html:7-29` | Keyboard users lose track of focus position. | Add a visible `:focus-visible` outline (e.g. `2px solid var(--accent2)`) on all interactive elements. |
| Low | Reduced-motion is half-respected. `index.html:106,128` correctly disable the traffic animation when `prefers-reduced-motion`, but the `map.flyTo`/`easeTo` camera animations (`index.html:187,224,232`) and the entire sim loop (`sim.html:137-146`) ignore the preference and still animate. | `index.html:187,224,232` (camera eases), `sim.html:137-146` (rAF loop, no REDUCE check) | Users with vestibular sensitivity still get camera swoops and a looping crash animation. | Gate `flyTo`/`easeTo` `duration` to 0 and the sim's auto-loop to a static end-state when `prefers-reduced-motion`. | Low | The sim never draws the satellite context it ships. `data/sat_corridor.png` (802 KB) is bundled but `sim.html` never references it; the "over real satellite imagery" promise is unmet and the asset is dead weight on load. | `data/sat_corridor.png` present; no reference in `sim.html` (no `Image`/`drawImage`/`sat_corridor`) | Wasted bytes + reinforces the overclaim above. | Either draw the PNG as the road backdrop in `drawScene`, or drop the asset and the "satellite" claim. |

## Observability findings (severity-ranked)

| Severity | Finding | Evidence (file:line) | Impact | Concrete fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High | No data-version / "as of" / OSM-snapshot date anywhere on the live page. The README says "live OpenStreetMap, June 2026" (`README.md:51`), but the app itself shows only "open data · 2026" in the brand chip — a visitor cannot tell which OSM snapshot or pull date the scores reflect, which is essential for a civic-data tool whose numbers change as OSM is edited. | `index.html:104` (brand chip: "Speed Safety Score · open data · 2026" — no date), `cities.json` has no `generated_at`/`osm_snapshot` field, methodology page `methodology.html:71-75` describes the source but states no snapshot date | Numbers can drift from OSM with no way for a user (or you) to know how stale the displayed data is. Undermines reproducibility and trust. | Stamp a `generated_at` (and the Overpass query date) into `cities.json` at build time; render "OSM data pulled {date}" in the footer and the methodology page. Per the data-integrity rules, the pull date is a sourced fact that belongs on the page. |
| High | A failed data fetch produces no console signal and no surfaced error — the app is unobservable when it breaks. There is zero `console.log`/`console.error`/`console.warn` in `index.html`, and the unguarded fetches (see UX-Critical above) reject silently. You cannot diagnose a production data-path break from the browser console or from user reports. | `index.html` — grep confirms no `console.*` and no `catch` anywhere; `index.html:182-184` (unguarded `Promise.all` fetches) | When (not if) a geojson path or CDN breaks, there's no breadcrumb in the console and no user-visible error; debugging starts from zero. | Add `.catch(err => { console.error('city load failed', key, err); showPanelError(key); })` to `loadCity`; log the resolved counts on success too so the console confirms which snapshot loaded. |
| Medium | No client-side telemetry or even a lightweight load-success marker. There's no analytics, no `window.__loaded` flag, no timing of how long the 5.5 MB fetch took — so you have no visibility into real-world load performance or whether visitors are hitting the slow-load abandonment problem. | `index.html` (no analytics, no `performance.mark`, no timing); `window.__map`/`window.__cur`/`window.__cur` exist for the e2e harness (`index.html:122,189`) but capture no health/timing | You can't tell whether mobile users are bouncing on load, or which city/data file is slow. | Add a minimal `performance.now()` delta around the `Promise.all` and `console.info` it; optionally a privacy-respecting counter. The e2e globals are a good hook — extend them with `window.__loadMs`. |
| Medium | The CDN dependency on `unpkg.com` for MapLibre is a single point of failure with no fallback or integrity check. If unpkg is down or the pinned 4.7.1 asset is pulled, the whole map fails to construct with only a generic browser error. | `index.html:12-13` (maplibre-gl CSS+JS from `unpkg.com`, no SRI, no fallback) | A third-party outage takes the site down with no graceful message. | Add `onerror` on the script tag that shows a "map library failed to load" message, or self-host the pinned MapLibre build (the CSP already allows `'self'`). Consider SRI hashes. |
| Low | The sim's only failure path is swallowed silently. `corridor.json` is fetched in a try/catch (good), but the catch is empty (`catch(e){}`) — if the file is missing in production the sim silently falls back to hardcoded P. Sanchez values with no log, so a data regression there is invisible. | `sim.html:54` (`catch(e){}` — empty) | A broken corridor.json is undetectable; the sim looks fine but may show stale/wrong corridor copy. | Log the fallback: `catch(e){console.warn('corridor.json missing, using defaults',e);}`. |
| Low | No version/build stamp on the assets or in the HTML to correlate a live bug with a deploy. The cache headers are short (`max-age=300`, `vercel.json`) which is fine, but nothing on the page identifies the build/commit, so a user report ("the map is wrong") can't be tied to a deploy. | `vercel.json` (cache headers only), no build/commit meta in `index.html` head | Hard to correlate reports with deploys. | Inject the git SHA / build date as a `<meta name="build">` at deploy time and log it on load. |

## Scores

**UX: 62 / 100.**
The design is genuinely good-looking and the thesis ("is the limit appropriate," not "do drivers speed") is clear and well-written. It loses points for: no loading state on a 5.5 MB payload (Critical), a silent-freeze on fetch failure (Critical), a 3D-tilt-by-default that is decoration not information on flat line data, animated "traffic" that is `Math.random()` particles labeled as real traffic, mouse-only keyboard-inoperable road rows and toggle buttons with no `aria-pressed`, red/green-only sim outcomes for colorblind users, a single thin mobile breakpoint, and README/button copy that overclaims a 2D canvas sim as "3D on real satellite." Strong bones, real accessibility and honesty gaps.

**Observability: 38 / 100.**
The methodology and sourcing are transparent (a real strength), but the running app is close to a black box: zero console logging, fetch failures that don't surface to the user or the console, no data-version / OSM-snapshot date on the page despite the data changing as OSM is edited, no load timing or telemetry, and a single-CDN dependency with no fallback. The one bright spot is the sim's try/catch — but its catch is empty. Most of these are a few lines each to fix.
